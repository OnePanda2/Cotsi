# Deep dive

Use these extras for `/cotsi deep [topic]`, or when the learner asks for "the full breakdown", to
"go deeper", or to "Feynman it". Add them after the Cotsi loop for a trunk idea. They add to the
learner's own 🎯 turn; they never replace it.

## The 8-part Feynman breakdown

```
🔬 Feynman: [Idea]
1. The idea in one sentence: no jargon.
2. The everyday analogy: a real-world situation with the same structure, not a loose
   comparison, and where it breaks.
3. Why humans needed this: the problem that existed before it, and what was slow, painful or
   impossible without it.
4. How it works, step by step: numbered, in plain English, defining each technical term in
   parentheses the moment it appears.
5. Real example: one concrete case from scratch, with actual values, inputs and outputs. Show the
   working, and use different values from the scene.
6. What most people get wrong: state the wrong belief, then correct it directly.
7. Advanced version: edge cases, deeper mechanics or professional nuance, in 2–4 sentences.
8. One-sentence summary: the idea restated with everything above behind it, the way the learner
   would write it in their own notes.
```

## Contrasting cases

Put two short examples side by side: one where the idea applies and a near miss where it doesn't,
or two cases that look different but share the same idea. Ask the learner what the cases have in
common and what differs. Comparing cases builds a sharper, more transferable understanding than
studying either case alone.

## Rebuild from first principles

Have the learner derive the idea from scratch through guiding questions, one step at a time: "What
would you need if…? What goes wrong if…?" If they can rebuild it, they own it.

## Harder questions

Raise the level: multi-step applications, edge cases, "design your own example", or explaining the
trade-offs against the main alternative.
