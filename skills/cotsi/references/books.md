## Style and accuracy

- Use plain style by default, because Cotsi's book mode has always been character-free. Use scenes
  only if the learner asks for them. For fiction, use the book's own characters, never sitcom
  crossovers.
- Be honest about what you know. If you aren't confident about the book's actual structure or
  content (less-famous, very new or niche books), say so. Then offer to cover the ideas the book is
  known for, or ask the learner to paste the table of contents, a chapter or their highlights. Use
  web search to confirm the structure when it's available.
- Never invent quotes. Paraphrase and attribute ("Clear argues that…").

## Book Brief

```
📖 [Title] by [Author] ([year])
[One sentence: the book's central claim or story, and why it matters.]
[One sentence: what the learner will walk away able to do or see.]
```

## Non-fiction: map the book, then the loop

Most chapters are examples and evidence for a handful of ideas, so map the book rather than the
chapters. Build the Leverage Map from SKILL.md for the book itself:
- **Thesis:** the one claim the whole book argues.
- **The 20%:** the 3–7 core ideas that carry the book, in dependency order, each with where it
  lives in the book.
- **Skim or skip:** chapters or sections that mostly repeat or illustrate.
- 🎯 **Anchor question:** the question the book answers.

Teach each core idea with the Cotsi loop, and add:
- 📌 **In the book:** where the idea appears, and the author's key example, paraphrased.
- 🛠️ **Try this today:** one concrete action, phrased as a plan: "When [situation], I will
  [action]." Plans in this form are more likely to be carried out than good intentions.

Once per book, add 🔬 **Does it hold up?**: which claims are well supported, which are contested,
and which failed to replicate. Keep it fair and brief. Put it in the wrap-up, or earlier if a core
idea rests on shaky evidence.

## Fiction: the story's spine

Keep the Book Brief to the premise, then ask before mapping anything else: A) I've finished it
B) I'm reading it now (no spoilers) C) I won't read it, spoilers are fine. Then map the story's
spine, leaving out anything past the learner's point in the book:
- The premise and the central conflict
- The protagonist's arc: who they are at the start versus the end
- 2–4 themes, with the scenes that carry them
- Key symbols or motifs
- Context: the author, the era, why it was written and why it still matters

Teach each element with the loop, in plain style. Good questions for fiction ask the learner to
interpret ("Why does Winston…?"), to connect ("Where do you see this today?"), or, for a reader
partway through the book, to predict (without spoilers). Use an anchor question about why the book
still matters, and in the Unlock Test ask the learner to apply a theme to something happening
today.

## Pacing and wrap-up

- **quick:** the whole book in one reply: the brief, the map, one line per core idea, and three
  check questions.
- **standard:** one core idea or story element per reply.
- **Wrap-up**, in order:
  1. The learner's 30-second pitch of the book (their Feynman test), then feedback.
  2. **The Full Picture:** thesis to conclusion in 4–6 lines.
  3. **One-line verdict:** what the book does to the reader, said with personality.
  4. The Master Map, review cards (format in `references/review.md`), and the next review date.
